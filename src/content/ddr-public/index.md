
# DDR public

The DDR Digital Archive is part of an ongoing doctoral research project at the Royal College of Art (RCA), investigating the history and contribution of its Department of Design Research between 1965 and 1985. The archive brings together information about the department's people, projects, publications and research activities, making historical material easier to discover, explore and connect. This guide introduces the digital archive and explains how to use its public-facing application.

## Historical background

The Department of Design Research (DDR), under the direction of Professor Bruce Archer, played an important role in the development of design research in Britain. Between 1965 and 1985, the department brought together researchers and collaborators working across design methods, education, healthcare, disability, computing, industrial design and policy. Their work explored fundamental questions about the nature of design, how designers develop knowledge, and how design research might contribute to wider society. This history includes the Design Education Unit (DEU), which developed approaches to design education and contributed to the department's broader research culture.

## About the research

The digital archive forms part of Graham Newman's practice-led PhD at the Royal College of Art. The research examines how surviving records can help reconstruct the DDR's activities, relationships and contribution to design knowledge. Historical material relating to the department is dispersed across collections held by the RCA and the Victoria and Albert Museum (V&A). Some records are incomplete, inconsistently described or difficult to access. The project investigates how digital methods can make these materials more accessible while preserving their historical context, provenance and uncertainty.

## Purpose of the digital archive

The public application provides a way to explore information about the department and investigate relationships between its people, projects and activities.

It is intended to support:

- Discovery of historical records and related information.
- Exploration of the DDR's people, projects and research activities.
- Investigation of relationships between records.
- Access to descriptive information about archival material.
- Further historical and design research.

The archive is a developing research resource rather than a definitive or complete account of the department.

## Scope and limitations

The digital archive represents material identified, described and incorporated into the research to date. It does not contain every surviving DDR document, nor should the absence of a record be taken as evidence that a person, activity or event did not exist. Descriptions and relationships may develop as further archival investigation, documentary evidence and oral history research become available. The application should therefore be understood as an evolving means of exploring historical evidence, rather than a final interpretation of DDR history.
